SOFT CONSONANTS

The letters C and G have two sounds each. The hard sound (as in cat or gap) is the default. But when C or G is followed by E, I, or Y, it often becomes soft: C sounds like /s/ and G sounds like /j/.

The Soft C and G Rule

HOW IT WORKS
C + G soften before E, I, or Y
Soft C = /s/
C before E, I, or Y
CE CI CY
Soft G = /j/
G before E, I, or Y
GE GI GY

Hard C says /k/ (as in cat, cup, clip). Before E, I, or Y, C softens to /s/ (as in city, rice, cycle). Hard G says /g/ (as in gap, got, gum). Before E, I, or Y, G often softens to /j/ (as in gem, giraffe, cage). Watch out: soft G is not as reliable as soft C. Some G+E/I/Y combinations stay hard (e.g. get, give, gear).

Exceptions

WATCH OUT
Words that don't soften as expected
get, give

Even though G is followed by E or I, these words keep the hard G sound (/g/). Soft G before E, I, or Y is very common but not 100% guaranteed: especially for short common words like get, give, gear, girl, gift.

magic, logic

These end in -IC (not -ICE or -ICE). The C makes the /k/ sound here, not /s/. When C comes before a consonant or at the very end of a word (with no following vowel), it says /k/ even if the preceding vowel was I.

cat vs. cent

This pair shows the rule in action: cat has C before A, so it's hard (/k/). Cent has C before E, so it's soft (/s/). Both are correct: the following vowel decides which sound you use.

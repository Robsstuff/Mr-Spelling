PUNCTUATION & SPELLING

The apostrophe of possession shows that something belongs to someone. Ask: "Who owns what? Don't leave the reader guessing!" For a singular owner, add 's. For a plural owner ending in S, add just '. For irregular plurals (plurals that don't end in S), add 's again.

The Possessive Apostrophe Rule

HOW IT WORKS
Who Owns What? Ask Before You Apostrophise!
Step 1: Find the owner (the person or thing that possesses something).
Step 2: Is the owner singular or plural?
  • Singular owner (one person/thing): add 's: the cat's tail, Jordan's bag, the dog's bowl.
  • Plural owner ending in S (more than one): add ' after the S: the teachers' staffroom, the dogs' toys, the babies' bottles.
  • Irregular plural owner (plurals that don't end in S): add 's: the children's books, the men's shoes, the sheep's wool.

Common Mistakes

WATCH OUT
Apostrophe traps to avoid
its vs it's

It's (apostrophe) = it is or it has. Its (no apostrophe) = belonging to it (possessive). The dog wagged its tail (possession, no apostrophe). It's raining (it is raining, apostrophe). This is one of the most common spelling errors in English.

plural vs possessive

Never use an apostrophe to make a word plural. apples (plural): no apostrophe. apple's skin (possessive): apostrophe. apple's never means "more than one apple." The apostrophe shows ownership, not quantity.

names ending in S

For names ending in S (like James, Chris), both James's and James' are accepted. Most style guides prefer adding 's for one-syllable names: James's book. For multi-syllable names ending in S (e.g. Jesus'), the apostrophe-only form is more common.

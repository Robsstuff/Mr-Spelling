UFLI Lesson 43

When a vowel is followed by a double L, it doesn't always sound the way you expect. This rap explores three common spelling patterns: -ALL (like in ball and wall), -OLL (like in roll and troll), and -ULL (like in dull and skull). Each section spells out the words letter by letter so students can see and hear the pattern. This song links to UFLI Lesson 43.

The Rule

Final Double L Changes the Sound of the Vowel
A + LL
→ "aw" sound
tall, small, ball
O + LL
→ "ole" sound
roll, troll, scroll
U + LL
→ "ull" sound
dull, gull, skull

When the letters A, O, or U come right before a double L at the end of a word, the vowel makes a different sound than you might expect. The double L steals the vowel's regular sound and replaces it. A says "aw", O says "ole", and U says "ull".

Exceptions to the Rule

These words do not follow the pattern
doll

Even though it ends in -OLL, the O says the short /o/ sound (like in "hot"), not the long /oʊ/ sound (like in "pole" or "roll"). Compare: roll says "ole": doll does not.

shall

Ends in -ALL but the A says the short /a/ sound (like in "hat"), not the /aw/ sound (like in "ball" or "tall").

full / pull / bull

-ULL words mostly say the /ʊ/ sound (like "foot") when the vowel is U, rather than the /ʌ/ sound in "dull" and "skull". Both sounds are common: students may notice that not all -ULL words sound exactly the same.

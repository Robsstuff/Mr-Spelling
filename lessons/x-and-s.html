S Never Follows X

Spell with XC or XES to Keep X and S Apart

In English, X and S are never written next to each other. When a word uses the /ks/ sound followed by /s/, you spell it with XC instead: excite, exceed, except. When a word ending in X becomes plural, you add ES, not just S: box, boxes. The E acts as a buffer to keep X and S apart.

The Rule: No XS

X and S are never written next to each other in English
XC
/ks/ + /s/ sound
excite, exceed, except
XES
plural of X words
box, boxes; fox, foxes

You will never see XS in an English word. There are two situations where you need to know this. First: when a word like excite or exceed has the /ks/ sound followed by another /s/ sound, the S is replaced by C. Soft C makes the /s/ sound before E, I, or Y, so XC gives you the /ks/ + /s/ combination without putting X and S together. Second: when a word ending in X becomes plural, English adds ES instead of just S. The E keeps X and S from touching. Say box then boxes: the word already has the XES sound built in.

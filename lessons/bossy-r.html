R-CONTROLLED VOWELS

Watch the Video

When the letter R comes after a vowel, it changes the vowel's sound completely. That's why R is called Bossy R — it bosses the vowel and makes it say something new. A+R = /ar/ (car), O+R = /or/ (fork), and E/I/U + R = /er/ (fern, girl, turn).

HOW IT WORKS

Bossy R

Vowel + R = a Completely New Sound
AR
/ar/ sound
car, barn, star
OR
/or/ sound
fork, corn, sport
ER
/er/ sound
fern, her, term
IR
/er/ sound
girl, bird, third
UR
/er/ sound
turn, fur, burn

When R follows a vowel, R bosses the vowel and changes its sound. A says "ah" normally, but A+R says "ar" (as in car). O says "oh" normally, but O+R says "or" (as in fork). ER, IR, and UR all make the same /er/ sound — that is the tricky part! You must learn which spelling each word uses.

WATCH OUT

Tricky Bossy R Words

Things that can confuse you
ER / IR / UR

All three make exactly the same sound: /er/. Compare: fern, girl, turn — they all sound the same in the stressed syllable but are spelled differently. You must memorise which spelling each word uses; the sound alone won't tell you.

four / for

Four (the number) uses OUR but still makes the /or/ sound. Not all /or/ sounds are spelled O+R — OUR (four, your), OAR (board, roar), and even ORE (more, store) can make the same sound.

war, warm

When W comes before AR, the sound changes from /ar/ to /or/: war, warm, wart, ward. The W softens the vowel. This is a known exception cluster to memorise.

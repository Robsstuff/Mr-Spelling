The Rule

HOW IT WORKS
Use CK After a Short Vowel in a One-Syllable Word
short vowel + CK → /k/ sound

When you hear the /k/ sound at the end of a one-syllable word and there is a short vowel right before it, use CK. The short vowel signals that CK is needed, not just K or C alone. Think: back, check, kick, lock, duck: short vowel then CK every time.

When NOT to Use CK

WATCH OUT
CK is not always the answer for /k/
like / bike / rake

After a long vowel, use just K (with silent E). The vowel is long, so CK is not needed. like, bike, bake, rake, spoke, fluke all end in K, not CK.

milk / silk / bulk

After a consonant, use just K. When a consonant sits between the vowel and the /k/ sound, use K alone. milk, silk, hulk, bulk, bank, think.

cat / cup / king / kit

At the start of a word, use C or K, never CK. cat, cup, coat use C. king, kit, keep use K. CK is only for the END of a word after a short vowel.

HOW IT WORKS

The Rule

Take Two Words — Remove Letters — Add an Apostrophe
word 1 + word 2 → word 1 ' short 2

To make a contraction: 1) Take two words and push them together. 2) Remove some letters from the second word. 3) Put an apostrophe (') exactly where those letters were removed. The apostrophe is a placeholder that says "letters are missing here." Example: I am → I'm (the "a" is removed and replaced with an apostrophe).

WATCH OUT

Tricky Contractions

These contractions can trip you up
won't

won't = will not — but the spelling changes from "will" to "wo". This is a historical quirk; the word "will" was once spelled "woll". The apostrophe marks where the "o" from "not" was removed, but the unusual spelling must simply be memorised.

it's vs its

it's (with apostrophe) = it is or it has. its (no apostrophe) = belonging to it. The apostrophe means "letters missing," NOT possession. This is the most common apostrophe mistake in English!

they're / their / there

they're = they are. their = belonging to them. there = a place. Three words that sound identical but are spelled differently and mean different things. Only they're is a contraction.

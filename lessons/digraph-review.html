CK, SH, CH, and TH (/θ/ and /ð/): Two Letters, One Sound

A digraph is two letters that work together to make one single sound. This song reviews four key digraphs: CK says /k/ (as in duck), SH says /sh/ (as in ship), CH says /ch/ (as in chop), and TH makes two different sounds: /θ/ as in thumb (voiceless) and /ð/ as in this (voiced). In every case, the two letters act as one unit.

Four Digraphs, Five Sounds

/θ/ voiceless
thumb, think, three
TH
/ð/ voiced

A digraph is two letters that combine to make one new sound. You cannot hear each letter separately: read the pair as one unit. CK always says /k/ and only appears at the end of a syllable after a short vowel (duck, back, clock). SH says /sh/ and can appear at the start or end of a word (ship, wish). CH says /ch/ and also appears in both positions (chop, lunch). TH has two separate sounds: the voiceless /θ/ (as in thumb, think, three, with no buzz in your throat) and the voiced /ð/ (as in this, that, them, where you feel a gentle buzz). These are different sounds and they appear in different words, so it helps to learn each group separately.

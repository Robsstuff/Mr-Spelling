The Four Vowel Types

HOW IT WORKS
A E I O U: Four Ways Vowels Can Sound
Short
Vowels say their quick sound: /a/ /e/ /i/ /o/ /u/.
Examples: cat, bed, sit, hop, bug
Long
Vowels say their alphabet name: /ay/ /ee/ /eye/ /oh/ /you/.
Examples: cake, feet, kite, rope
Schwa /ə/
Vowels get lazy and say "uh" in unstressed syllables.
Examples: sofa, panda, lemon
Bossy R
R changes the vowel sound: ar, er, ir, or, ur.
Examples: barn, bird, corn

Every vowel in every English word fits into one of these four categories. When you can identify which type you are dealing with, you can work out how to say: or spell: the word.

Tips for Vowel Sounds

IMPORTANT TO KNOW
Things that can trick you
vowel teams

Two vowels together can make a long vowel sound (like AI in rain, EE in feet, OA in boat). These are called vowel teams or digraphs. They are still "long" vowels: just spelled with two letters.

same letter, different sounds

The same vowel can make different sounds in different words. The letter A can be short (cat), long (cake), schwa (sofa), or R-controlled (car). Context and spelling patterns tell you which one to use.

silent E rule

A magic E at the end of a word often makes the vowel before it long: cap → cape, bit → bite, hop → hope. This is one of the most common ways long vowels are spelled in English.

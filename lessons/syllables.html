WORD STRUCTURE

Watch the Video

A syllable is a beat in a word. Clap or chop your hand for each beat to count them. Every syllable has at least one vowel sound in it. That rule never breaks: no vowel sound = no syllable. Words from the video: elephant, chicken, cookie, poodle, watermelon, calculator, turtle, kangaroo, electricity, hippopotamus, monkey, television.

HOW IT WORKS

The Syllable Rule

A Syllable is a Beat — Every Syllable Has a Vowel Sound
EL • E • PHANT
3 syllables
CHICK • EN
2 syllables
KAN • GA • ROO
3 syllables
WA • TER • MEL • ON
4 syllables

To count syllables: 1) Say the word out loud. 2) Clap or chop your hand for each beat you can feel. 3) Count the claps = the number of syllables. Each syllable must contain a vowel sound (A, E, I, O, U, or sometimes Y). If you can't hear a vowel sound, it's not a syllable.

TIPS

Tips for Syllables

Things to remember
silent E

A silent E at the end of a word is still a syllable helper, but because it is silent (no sound), the final E does NOT count as its own syllable. cake has 1 syllable (not 2), even though it ends in E.

vowel teams

Two vowel letters together (like OO in poodle, or AI in rain) usually make one vowel sound — so they count as one syllable between them, not two. Poodle = 2 syllables: POO-dle.

Y as a vowel

Y can act as a vowel and carry a syllable on its own. In butterfly, the final Y makes the /eye/ sound and counts as a syllable: BUT-ter-FLY (3 syllables). In happy, the final Y makes the /ee/ sound: HAP-py (2 syllables).

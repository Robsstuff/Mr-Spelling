The most common sound is /ch/: two letters making one sound, heard at the start of chip, chop, chat and at the end of beach, much, lunch. This is the sound the song teaches. CH can also say /k/ in words that come from Greek, such as school, echo, chemist, choir and stomach. And CH can say /sh/ in words borrowed from French, such as chef, machine, brochure and parachute. The word's origin is the main clue to which sound CH makes.

Watch Out

Things that can confuse you
TCH

When the /ch/ sound comes right after a short vowel at the end of a syllable, it is usually spelled TCH, not CH: match, catch, fetch, witch, kitchen. TCH and CH make the exact same /ch/ sound. The rule is: short vowel before /ch/ at the end = use TCH. TCH has its own song and its own lesson.

school, stomach, choir

These words have CH but it makes the /k/ sound. This happens in words that came into English from Greek. The CH in school, stomach, echo, chemist, choir, chaos and chrome all say /k/. If a word looks scientific or medical, CH is often /k/.

chef, machine, brochure

These words have CH but it makes the /sh/ sound. This happens in words borrowed from French. The CH in chef, machine, brochure, parachute, champagne and chalet all say /sh/. French-origin words are often connected to food, fashion, or travel.
